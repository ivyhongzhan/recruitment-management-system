- **Research period:** 2023.
- **Original title:** *Differences between the Use of Social Media by Chinese Students in the UK and Their Home Country's Mainstream Media Culture*.
- **Supervisor:** Daniel Robins.

3. A user-provided research account reports recording, transcription, translation, and interview lengths of 30–60 minutes. The full dissertation, transcripts, and final codebook were **not available for independent verification** in this build.

This document is a public-facing methodological summary, **not** an archived copy of the original methods chapter.

## Research design as reported

- **Design:** qualitative, interview-led inquiry about social media practices and cultural identity among Chinese students studying in the UK.
- **Sample:** 15 interviewees. Do not impute gender, age, recruitment method, sampling frame, platform-use frequency, or saturation criteria.
- **Data handling reported:** interviews were recorded and transcribed; translations and organization were completed by the researcher; thematic coding was supported by NVivo and Excel.
- **Topics reported in the available CV/research description:** cross-platform communication, language and participation, evaluations of information credibility, and perceived differences in platform norms or content controls.

### Analytic workflow (reported at a high level)

1. Conduct semi-structured interviews addressing platform experiences and adaptation.
2. Transcribe and prepare materials for analysis; work across languages when needed.
3. Assign qualitative codes and iteratively organize higher-level themes.
4. Interpret observed accounts in relation to cultural adaptation and platform context.

Detailed coding passes, inter-coder procedures, reflexive memos, code definitions and audit-trail dates have **not** been independently reconstructed. The list above should not be treated as a line-by-line original research protocol.

## What the work does and does not demonstrate

**Supported portfolio claims:** design and administration of qualitative interviews; thematic interpretation of cross-platform experiences; practice in research documentation and NVivo-supported coding.

**Unsupported without additional documentation:** exact theme frequencies, participant quotations, generalized prevalence estimates, causal effects, coding reliability coefficients, and claims of completed new usability testing.

## Data access and privacy

The original interview recordings/transcripts, participants' contact data, potentially identifying social-media posts and any consent documentation are **not** included. Participant confidentiality takes priority over making a public code-and-data repository. The original ethics application is not distributed because it contains personal identifiers and is not proof of an approval decision.

## Additional artifact included

`QUALITATIVE_CODEBOOK_TEMPLATE.csv` is a **blank planning template created in 2026**, not the 2023 original codebook. It provides an inspectable schema without inventing interview excerpts or codes.

## Possible future evaluation (not conducted)

A follow-up HCI study could evaluate cross-platform information verification tasks using informed consent, privacy review, a specified sampling frame, carefully defined outcome measures, and a preregistered/transparent analysis plan as appropriate. These are future possibilities, **not** findings from this MSc dissertation.

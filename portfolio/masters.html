MSc dissertation · University of York · Research completed 2023

Differences between the Use of Social Media by Chinese Students in the UK and Their Home Country's Mainstream Media Culture

An interview-based inquiry into cross-platform communication, perceptions of media environments, and cultural identity among Chinese international students studying in the UK.

MSc Social Media and Management · Supervisor: Dr Daniel Robins

Research question

How does social media use shape cultural identity and belonging?

The dissertation ethics application states the overarching question as: “How does the use of social media by Chinese students studying in the UK impact their sense of cultural identity and belonging?”

The study explored students' navigation of communication platforms and perceived differences between British and Chinese media environments.

Research design and analysis

01 · Data collection15 semi-structured interviews with Chinese students in the UK.
02 · PreparationAudio recordings were transcribed and translated for analysis.
03 · InterpretationQualitative coding and thematic analysis supported by NVivo and Excel.

I designed and conducted the interviews, recorded and prepared the materials, and coded them thematically in NVivo with Excel-supported organization. I also analyzed social-media posts.

Main findings

  • Cross-platform communication: how participants used different platforms to sustain and adapt social connections.
  • Language and participation: barriers encountered when navigating social and informational environments.
  • Credibility checking: how students evaluated information across platforms and media contexts.
  • Platform conditions: perceived differences in communication norms and content restrictions.
These qualitative themes describe participants’ experiences rather than population prevalence. Participant materials remain private; the completed dissertation and original coding exports are not available for review.

Materials and later software

The surviving ethics-application draft records the planned study, not a completed approval decision. No participant audio, transcripts, identifiable posts, or quotations are distributed here.

A methods note summarizes the study. The blank codebook template was created in 2026 and is not the original codebook. A separate Python companion illustrates coding checks on fictional examples.

MSc dissertation · University of York · Research completed 2023

Differences between the Use of Social Media by Chinese Students in the UK and Their Home Country's Mainstream Media Culture

An interview-based inquiry into cross-platform communication, perceptions of media environments, and cultural identity among Chinese international students studying in the UK.

MSc Social Media and Management · Supervisor: Dr Daniel Robins

Research question

How does social media use shape cultural identity and belonging?

The dissertation ethics application states the overarching question as: “How does the use of social media by Chinese students studying in the UK impact their sense of cultural identity and belonging?”

The study explored students' navigation of communication platforms and perceived differences between British and Chinese media environments.

Research design and analysis

01 · Data collection15 semi-structured interviews with Chinese students in the UK.
02 · PreparationAudio recordings were transcribed and translated for analysis.
03 · InterpretationQualitative coding and thematic analysis supported by NVivo and Excel.

The study was qualitative, not a numerical survey or an automated text-mining experiment. The site does not assign invented frequencies or quantitative effect sizes to themes.

Topics identified in the research summary

  • Cross-platform communication: how participants used different platforms to sustain and adapt social connections.
  • Language and participation: barriers encountered when navigating social and informational environments.
  • Credibility checking: how students evaluated information across platforms and media contexts.
  • Platform conditions: perceived differences in communication norms and content restrictions.
These are summarized themes documented in available CV/project notes, not quotations or counts independently reconstructed from interview transcripts. The full dissertation and coding exports were not available for this portfolio build.

Methodological contribution and limits

This case illustrates experience with research design, interviewing, transcription, translation, and interpretive coding. It is relevant to HCI and Information Science because it concerns people's situated interactions with information platforms and the judgments they make across contexts.

The sample is small and purposive details have not been verified from the original dissertation. The findings are not population estimates. Without the underlying transcripts and analytic log, a third party cannot independently re-run the original qualitative coding.

Research ethics and transparency

No interview audio, transcripts, identifiable posts, or participant quotations are distributed here. A historical ethics application was located, but it does not by itself establish an approval decision. No approval status is claimed.

Supporting documentation prepared for this public portfolio consists of a methods and evidence note and a blank codebook template. The template is not presented as the original analysis.
